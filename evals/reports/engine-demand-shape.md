Case instances priced by the oracle: 6,561 (2,870 shortage, 3,691 warning) at 28 days; 6,747 (2,757 / 3,990) at 56; 6,683 (2,401 / 4,282) at 91: a longer window turns shortage cases into warnings rather than closing them.

## Reading

- Demand is intermittent: a few order days a month, often one large order. A 28-day mean turns one order into a daily rate that the next month does not repeat, so most shortage cases (76 %) and almost all warnings (96 %) needed nothing.
- Variability as a spread of daily quantities does not separate the warnings that went below zero from those that did not; the number of order days and the horizon do separate shortage cases.
- Lengthening the window cuts the regret by about half (993 → 524 per case) but also the recall (30 % → 21 %): it trades precision for recall rather than telling a real stock-out from a false one. The options in record 002 are about that separation.
